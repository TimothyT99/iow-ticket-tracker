FAQ

What do the prices on this site mean?
Every price shown is an all-in estimate — the seller's asking price plus the ~15.9% Twickets buyer fee added at checkout. That makes resale prices directly comparable to a face-value purchase, where fees are usually bundled in.


What's the difference between camping and non-camping tickets?
None, for access — both are full weekend tickets with the same face value and the same Thursday–Sunday entry. The only difference is whether you get an on-site campsite pitch. On resale the two can drift apart in price (sellers often list non-camping a little cheaper), so both are tracked and badged separately. The headline stats and signals use camping tickets as the primary series.


What does "likely sold" mean on the Sold tab?
Twickets doesn't tell us when a ticket sells, so we infer it: when a listing that was there one snapshot is gone the next, it's logged as a disappearance. If it vanished at or below the Ticketmaster price it's flagged likely sold; if it was priced higher, it was more likely withdrawn or relisted. It's an estimate, not a confirmed sale — in 2026 only about a third of disappearances were at plausible sale prices.


How fresh is the data?
In the run-up to a festival the resale market is checked roughly every 15 minutes during UK waking hours, and the dashboard updates automatically. Between festivals it idles — when there's no event on sale you'll see a "not on sale yet" note, and tracking restarts on its own once the next year's tickets appear.


Twickets vs "Ticketmaster Verified Resale" — what's the difference?
They're two separate resale routes. Twickets is what this site monitors automatically (every listing, all day). Ticketmaster Verified Resale opened later in 2026 and is sampled by hand now and then — shown as occasional points on the trend chart so you can compare the two.


Why does it keep several years?
So each season can be compared against the last. Pick a year from the selector at the top right; completed seasons (like 2026) stay fully browsable as historical archives, and the long-term aim is to use past curves to read the current market.


Behind the scenes (for the maintainer): a Playwright scraper on GitHub Actions captures Twickets listings, commits them to JSON, and Netlify rebuilds the site automatically. To add a new year, drop its Twickets event ID + face values into public/data/events.json and tracking begins on the next run. Full setup, scheduling and data-format notes live in the repo's README.md.

Commentary & Evidence

About this commentary
This is the final 2026 retrospective, computed from the complete season — 2,243 snapshots, 27 Apr to 18 Jun 2026, captured every 15 minutes during UK hours. Every claim below is drawn from that data; the "hypothesis" labels carried during the season have now been settled.


What the 2026 data showed
• Prices fell into the festival — they didn't spike. Camping avg all-in peaked ~£416 in late May (just after the 15 May sell-out, around TM transfers opening 27 May), then eased to ~£350 in the final week: roughly £406 at 3–4 weeks out vs ~£352 in the last week.
• The cheapest window was the final 1–2 weeks. Median daily floor was ~£253 all-in inside the last 7 days (cheapest single ticket £227), vs ~£307–320 at 15–45 days out. The conventional "buy 4–6 weeks out" did not hold — later was cheaper.
• But cheap meant fast. 87% of listings that disappeared (405 of 463) vanished the same day they were last seen. Single-ticket floors bottomed around £227–255 all-in.
• Bundles & non-ticket passes are excluded. The camping figures ignore multi-item lots and passes that the old keyword classifier used to count — from a £58 campsite "facilities" pass and adult+infant lots to campervan-pitch passes up to £527. These had badly skewed the floor (and inflated the average), so they were removed and guarded against in future.
• Disappearance ≠ sale. Only ~38% of disappearances (175 of 463) were priced ≤ £320 all-in (plausible sale prices); the rest were more likely withdrawn or relisted.
• Supply dried up sharply at the end. Camping listings peaked at 28 (13 May), fell to 13 by 11 Jun and 9 on the eve, and reached 0 on festival day — only a few non-camping leftovers (£217–£345 all-in) remained.
• Non-camping ≈ camping overall. Season averages were near-identical (camping ~£383, non-camping ~£380), and the non-camping floor was the cheapest ticket on 41% of days. No consistent non-camping discount emerged.


Hypotheses — now settled by the full season
• Final-week price peak — not supported. Prices were flat-to-softer in the final week; the only uptick was the eve of the festival as supply collapsed, not a genuine demand spike.
• "Best buy window is 4–6 weeks out" — contradicted. In 2026 both the floor and the average were lowest in the final 1–2 weeks.
• Lineup-announcement spikes — still untested. Our data began after both 2026 announcements; carried over to test against 2027.
